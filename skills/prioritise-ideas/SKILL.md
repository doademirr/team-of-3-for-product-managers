Collaborative prioritisation of competing ideas. **Divergent** — there is no single right answer; the job is to sharpen the trade-off until the user can decide well, **never to pick for them**. Interview-heavy: many people think by writing, so expect a long conversation — the user dumps what they know, gives opinions, reacts. Absorb the waves; map each fragment to the right place; ask only about genuine gaps. Teaching layer throughout.

**What this is not:** it does not run a full discovery, user-research, or competitor teardown (those are separate skills that *feed* this one). It **consumes** evidence by default and only does **bounded, targeted verification** on load-bearing claims (Step 2).

## Step 0: The gate — every idea ties to a problem or goal

Start here, before any scoring. Establish the **problem(s) or goal(s)** on the table — e.g. "increase revenue 15%", "cut onboarding complaints", "reduce churn in month 2". Then: **every candidate idea must trace to one.** An idea with no problem or goal behind it ("just for fun") is a **bad idea** — flag it and park it; do not score it. Ideas exist to move a problem or a goal, nothing else.

## Step 1: Frame the field

- List the problems/goals, and under each, the candidate ideas that target it.
- Reflect the breakdown back and confirm before going deeper. (Teaching: this is the "problem → candidate solutions" shape from `PLAYBOOK.md`.)

## Step 2: Gather ideas + evidence — the long chat

For each idea, walk it with the user (absorb brain-dumps; don't march them back through what they've said):
- **What it is** and which problem/goal it serves.
- **The evidence behind it** — stats, data, customer reviews, support tickets, anything. **Tier each piece:** *Strong* (hard data / multiple independent sources) · *Moderate* (some evidence / single source) · *Assumption* (gut, anecdote, unverified). Say the tier plainly.
- **Flag evidence gaps** — an idea resting on an untiered claim is a hole, not a strength.

**Bounded verify/research (on-demand, not always-on).** Do a targeted check ONLY when a **load-bearing claim is unverified** (the idea's whole case rests on it) or an idea has **no evidence** and that gap will decide its rank. Then: web-search the specific claim, **2 independent sources** for anything high-consequence, report what you found — or flag "couldn't verify → treat as Assumption". Do not expand into a full research pass; retrieve the specific fact and conclude.

## Step 3: Agree the criteria — before scoring

Default to the frameworks in `PLAYBOOK.md`; confirm or adjust with the user so the criteria are explicit and agreed *before* any scoring (this prevents moving the goalposts once results appear):
- **RICE** — Reach × Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort. **Confidence is grounded in the evidence tier from Step 2** — a high score on an Assumption is not confident.
- **Value vs Complexity** — the quick-win / big-bet / fill-in / time-sink matrix.
- **Problem-phase scoring dims** (for problem-level choices) — frequency, severity, reach, addressability, commercial path, strategic fit.

Pick the framework(s) that fit the decision; don't force all three.

## Step 4: Compete the ideas — the matrix

Build an **options × criteria matrix**. For each idea:
- Score against the agreed criteria (+ evidence tier feeding Confidence).
- Its **strongest case** (one line) and its **key risk** (one line).
- **Tag the resources it needs**, from a fixed vocabulary so ideas are comparable: **design · frontend · backend · data · content · GTM** (add "other: …" only if genuinely none fit). Lightweight tags — no effort estimates, no capacity maths.

## Step 5: Cluster + frame the decision

- **Identify competition vs parallelism** by resource: ideas that need the **same** resource compete (roughly mutually exclusive now); ideas with **no shared resource** are parallelizable — they can run at once (the classic 1-design + 1-bug-fix + 1-content bundle). A judgment call on the tags, not a solver.
- **Name the crux** — what the decision actually turns on (the one variable that flips the ranking).
- **Offer the output shapes**, whichever the field supports:
  - a **single pick**,
  - a **primary + backups**, or
  - a **parallel set** of non-competing ideas.
- **Recommendation withheld or clearly marked optional.** State what you'd lean toward *only* if asked, and label it as the user's call — never present a winner as decided.

## Step 6: Hand back to the user

The frame is the deliverable; the judgment is theirs. Mark it **"decision-ready — for your call,"** show the matrix + crux + candidate shapes + what (if anything) is still needed to decide.

## Grounding rules
- **Never invent evidence.** Untiered = Assumption; missing = an open question, never fabricated prose.
- **Never pick the winner.** Divergent means the choice is the user's; withhold or clearly mark any lean as optional.
- **Ideas without a problem/goal are flagged, not scored** (Step 0).
- **Evidence tier is visible** at the point of scoring — a high score on an Assumption must show its weak footing.
